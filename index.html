Chapter II

Thesis

Thesis Statement

The Persian and Chinese governments’ ability to maintain peace and order within their civilizations through multiculturalism in soft power shows that humans will tolerate foreign rule as long as their identity is preserved.

What is soft power?

Soft power, created by American political scientist Joseph Nye, is a way for countries to gain cooperation from foreign parties without the need for military might.1 Many nations often use soft power as it is more diplomatic and requires less military control of the people. Soft power is also typically more efficient and cost-effective, as leveraging cultural, ideological, and other resources have a gentler effect on the citizens than being forced to cooperate. The goal of using soft power is for both parties to be satisfied. The government is content with the control or influence of citizens, and the people are content with the government’s support and flexibility.

Chapter III

Comparison

Both the modern Chinese and ancient Persian governments used soft power to peacefully influence rather than fully conquer foreign groups.


A method both governments used was fair and internationally appealing diplomacy. This means that their actions were not only fair to the people but also put the country in a good light on the global stage.2

During the invasion of Babylon, Cyrus the Great forbade anyone from frightening the civilians of Babylon, the city they were attempting to take over, and sought the welfare of the city and sacred centers. He then relieved them of their weariness and freed them from their service.3 Cyrus the Great provided the people with humanitarian aid, rebuilt the city he destroyed while conquering, and promised not to enslave them. Through his actions, he gained the trust of the Babylonian citizens and peacefully incorporated them into the Persian empire. The new citizens were still able to work for themselves, maintain self-governance to an extent, and worship their own gods, thus reinforcing the multiculturalist aspects of the Persian empire. Because of his generous actions, the Babylonians were extremely tolerant of Cyrus’s rule. Even though they were now technically Persian citizens, they still maintained their Babylonian culture, identity, and practices. Over time, the Persian empire’s ideologies, including Zoroastrianism, will seamlessly merge with the Babylonian religion, proving their tolerance. Persia’s large territorial footprint already made ruling difficult, so through soft power, the government can use the tolerance of the citizens to help suppress rebellion. Once the Persian empire gained a reputation, it also faced less resistance when conquering new territories, as the defenders knew the King would ensure fairness and flexibility.

The modern Chinese government also shows such soft power through its Belt and Road Initiative (BRI). China’s BRI mission is to create infrastructure to connect East Asia to the West and South, including Europe, the Middle East, and India. The project can improve efficiency, trade, logistics, foreign relations, and more. The BRI is often referred to as the modern Silk Road.2 Since China has historical conflicts with many of these countries, including European nations, the BRI shows China’s initiative to reform relationships. Through providing the infrastructure needed for the project, China shows its hospitality and pursuit of multiculturalism. Many of the nations China is building through are smaller, developing countries. Alongside the larger megaproject, China also funds many smaller projects, such as renewable energy initiatives, and funding support, in these developing countries. Not only can China benefit from the passageway, but citizens of these smaller countries can enjoy increased living standards and modernization. (https://www.cfr.org/china-digital-silk-road/ this image) The cooperation between larger and smaller countries is similar to the Persian empire, as the new territories they conquered were very tolerant of the new Persian rule. As a result, North African countries become eager to embrace new Chinese technologies and cultures. This eagerness then proves to the rest of the world the positive influence China has, therefore gaining the Chinese an advantage on the global stage.

Chapter IV

Contrast

The largest differences between the tolerance of Persian and modern Chinese soft power are their methods of implementation, scope of control, and their goals for control.

Two ways the Persians spread soft power were through religion and the economy. Although it is evident that they had high tolerances for other religions, the most dominant religion of the Empire was Zoroastrianism.1 A common practice of the Persian government was to allow conquered empires to keep their religion if they agreed to also embrace Zoroastrianism. Even though Zoroastrianism is a separate, monotheistic religion, it still imposes a strong influence on the makeup of developing cultures. For example, modern Islam and Christianity both have origins in Zoroastrianism. Although people around the ancient world were separated by religion and geography, they were all connected, having Zoroastrian roots in their religion. Not only is local identity still preserved, but aspects of a common Persian identity help the empire hold rule, as proven by its extensive history and positive relations with diverse communities. Alongside religion, the Persians’ coinage system and the Royal Road served as the backbone of its economic influence.2 The standardized Persian coin is the gold Daric.3 On the front of every coin was the picture of the Persian king. The Daric was used internationally, including empires not under the direct control of the King. Therefore, the Persian government ensures that traces of Persian influence can be found everywhere; the Persian king would always be with the people. With enough time, citizens who use the coin every day become connected with the King and look up to him. To further solidify the message that Persia is always with you, King Darius created the Royal Road. Similar to Roman roads, the Royal Road was a highly sophisticated highway system stretching across the empire. At its fastest, messengers could consistently travel across the entire empire in just nine days, regardless of the weather. The use of the Road allowed for extremely quick communication of news and information across the empire. Even the most remote places will be up to date with their governance. Issues in an area could be quickly transmitted to the government, and people could quickly receive solutions and change. Citizens everywhere will feel seen and cared for, leading to higher levels of loyalty, diminishing resistance, and proving the effectiveness of Persian soft power.

However, the modern Chinese government uses investments and technology unavailable to the Persians. Much of China’s soft power lies in the Digital Silk Road (DSR). The DSR is a project that provides support to Chinese exports, technological enterprises, and other high-tech areas, as well as investment into developing nations. Many prosperous nations have also signed memorandums of understanding, showing global interest in the DSR.4 Because China cannot directly conquer foreign nations, it must direct its soft power in other ways, including investment. Without the need to govern them, China can focus all its foreign efforts on ensuring influence. For example, China’s presence in North Africa is especially significant in the current society as China is one of the few, as well as the largest Asian country, investing in the region. Through China’s soft power in financial and technological support, it can not only ensure cooperation and loyalty but also decrease the African nations’ reliance on Western countries. China’s soft power is used not just to maintain tolerance in North Africa, but rather to combat the soft power of other countries. China continues to spread artificial intelligence models, social media platforms, and other forms of technology produced with a higher quality than competing nations. Additionally, Chinese scientists are also working with foreign scientists through technological exchanges sponsored by the government.5 When foreigners repeatedly use these Chinese products, they start to feel a Chinese identity in addition to their own. African governments, seeing that their people are content with Chinese products, will continue to promote and demand them, allowing China to hold a strong influence in these developing countries.

Even with these different methods, the Persian Empire was further disadvantaged as its influence was limited to within its borders. The Royal Road and Zoroastrianism were bound domestically, although the Persian currency was used in global markets. In order to influence more people, the empire would need to directly conquer new territories first. However, the Chinese government has the technology to influence countries outside of China. The DSR lets the government influence without the need for presence. Actions in trade, communication, and influence give the government the ability to induce Chinese culture and loyalties thousands of kilometers away.

Underlying motives are also a strong contrast between the two countries. The Persian goal for human tolerance was to maintain a peaceful and loyal nation. Without instant methods of enforcement over a large footprint, minimizing resistance was imperative for a strong and healthy country. The Chinese government, however, used tolerance as a tool to combat other countries' influence in developing countries. If the Persians used soft power defensively, the Chinese used it offensively. If China can secure influence, they also secure an investment in new markets in the future. Providing these countries with the high-end infrastructure ensures that the citizens and governments are happy with the Chinese by their side, and therefore overturns loyalty to them.

Chapter V

To What Extent?

The Prompt

"To what extent is an enduring, illuminating aspect of human nature represented in both an important example from the Ancient World as well as one from the 21st century?"

Not At All
Completely

The extent to which the human nature of tolerance is present in the modern world depends entirely on the purpose of soft power, whether it is used for the benefit of the citizens or to advance the interests of the nation. In the case of the Persian empire, tolerance is present to a very large extent due to the genuine use of soft power. In ancient Persia, soft power was used to the benefit of the multicultural territories that Persia had conquered. The government deliberately made sure that different cultural groups could still practice their own religions and keep their identities, but still seamlessly merge with Persian practices. The government also ensured that citizens all over the state were treated humanely and well, as seen by the Royal Roads providing relatively quick aid to anywhere in the empire. Due to the ethical use of soft power, citizens, including those of Babylonia, were extremely tolerant of Persian rule and minimized resistance.

However, the Chinese use of soft power resulted in tolerance to a far lesser extent. People of North Africa, as well as political scientists and analysts, saw through China’s ambitions to overpower Western influence.1 China’s soft power is not genuine, but is rather used as a tool to dominate other countries. Although the Chinese do promote their soft power as supporting and funding developing nations, their real goal to combat Western influence draws significant opposition internationally.

Luckily, Chinese soft power does have a positive side. Taking it out of the scope of North Africa, China still imposes a lot of healthy soft power through spreading Chinese culture. Culture is something that people around the world can relate to, and it is a healthy way of forming bonds in many communities. Whilst the extent of tolerance is often impaired due to the manipulative use of soft power in the modern world, there are still many examples of large tolerance mirroring the Persian empire when soft power is used genuinely.

Chapter VI

Conclusion

"Soft power can be beneficial to the people, but the ultimate decision of tolerance should be protected as a choice by the people."

— George Yang, 2026

Today, human tolerance to soft power can be found almost everywhere, even without people know it. Social media is filled with soft power, with influencers and companies trying to sell their content and products to you through videos, articles, advertisements, and other forms of media. Many users online are quite tolerant of, and usually enjoy, these micro soft powers, as they define other aspects of human nature such as hobbies and passions. Because of soft power, many people find things they enjoy that become part of their identity, which brings people together through culture. However, much like soft power used globally, human tolerance should not be a tool or leverage to control people. Even though human tolerance makes up a portion of one’s identity, soft power becomes dangerous when it seeks to eliminate other portions of that identity. As seen before in the Persian empire, genuine soft power leads to genuine tolerance; tolerance cannot be forced by governments and parties, but is rather a result of fair influence decided by the population. When soft power becomes negative, as some analysts argue in the Chinese actions, human tolerance is often forced and inauthentic, leading to higher chances of resistance. Overall, soft power can be beneficial to the people, but the ultimate decision of tolerance should be protected as a choice by the people. Therefore, it’s imperative for populations to decide how much influence they are willing to take, and therefore translate that to how much tolerance they will provide in return.
